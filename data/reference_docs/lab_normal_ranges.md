# Common Lab Value Normal Ranges (Adult Reference Ranges)

Blood Urea: 7-20 mg/dL. Elevated levels can indicate reduced kidney
function, dehydration, or high protein intake.

Serum Creatinine: 0.6-1.3 mg/dL. A waste product filtered by the kidneys;
persistently elevated levels are one of the primary indicators of reduced
kidney function.

Hemoglobin: 13.5-17.5 g/dL (male), 12.0-15.5 g/dL (female). Low hemoglobin
(anemia) is common in CKD, since the kidneys produce a hormone
(erythropoietin) that stimulates red blood cell production.

Sodium: 135-145 mEq/L. Kidneys regulate sodium balance; abnormal levels
can reflect kidney dysfunction, fluid balance issues, or dietary factors.

Potassium: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L. Kidneys are the primary regulator of potassium;
elevated potassium (hyperkalemia) is a significant concern in advanced
CKD, since it can affect heart rhythm.

Albumin (urine): Normally minimal to absent in urine. Persistent albumin
in urine (albuminuria) is an early marker of kidney damage, often
appearing before a drop in eGFR.

Specific Gravity: 1.005-1.030. Measures urine concentration; abnormal
values can reflect the kidney's reduced ability to concentrate or dilute
urine, a function that often declines in CKD.
