# CKD Stages (Based on eGFR)

Chronic Kidney Disease is commonly staged using estimated Glomerular
Filtration Rate (eGFR), measured in mL/min/1.73m^2.

Stage 1: eGFR 90 or above, with other evidence of kidney damage (e.g.
protein in urine). Kidney function is normal, but damage markers are present.

Stage 2: eGFR 60-89, with evidence of kidney damage. Mildly reduced
kidney function.

Stage 3a: eGFR 45-59. Mild to moderate reduction in kidney function.

Stage 3b: eGFR 30-44. Moderate to severe reduction in kidney function.

Stage 4: eGFR 15-29. Severe reduction in kidney function. Preparation for
possible dialysis or transplant typically begins around this stage.

Stage 5: eGFR below 15. Kidney failure. Dialysis or transplant is
typically required.

Serum creatinine rises as eGFR falls, since eGFR is calculated partly from
creatinine level, age, sex, and body size. A serum creatinine of 3.8 mg/dL
is well above the normal range (typically 0.6-1.3 mg/dL) and, depending on
age/sex/body size, commonly corresponds to an eGFR in the Stage 4 or Stage
5 range -- indicating severe kidney function loss, though the exact stage
requires the actual eGFR calculation, not creatinine alone.
