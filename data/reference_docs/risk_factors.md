# CKD Risk Factors

Diabetes Mellitus: The leading cause of CKD in many populations. Chronic
high blood sugar damages the small blood vessels in the kidneys over time.

Hypertension: The second most common cause of CKD. Sustained high blood
pressure damages kidney blood vessels and impairs filtration function.

Age: Kidney function naturally declines with age; risk of CKD increases
notably after age 60.

Family history: A family history of kidney disease increases individual
risk, suggesting a genetic component in some cases.

Cardiovascular disease: Conditions affecting the heart and blood vessels
(including coronary artery disease) are strongly associated with CKD,
since kidney and cardiovascular health are closely linked.

Recurrent urinary tract infections or obstructions: Repeated infections
or structural blockages can cause progressive kidney damage over time.

Obesity: Associated with increased risk of both diabetes and
hypertension, and may independently stress kidney function.

Smoking: Associated with faster progression of existing kidney disease
and increased cardiovascular risk, which compounds kidney strain.
